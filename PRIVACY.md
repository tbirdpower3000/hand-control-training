# Privacy and disclaimers

**The camera stays on your computer.** The training page asks for your webcam so a hand-tracking library (Google MediaPipe, running in your browser) can find your hand. The video is processed on your device and is never sent anywhere, recorded or saved. Nothing from the camera leaves your computer.

**Your eyes, for the camera models.** The page also runs Google MediaPipe's face tracker in your browser, only to measure where your eyes are in the picture and the gap between your pupils (that gap says how far you sit from the camera). Like the hand, it is processed on your computer and never sent, recorded or saved. What is saved in the profile is three calibration numbers per hand, worked out during the Reach step: how far your hand is held in front of your face (cm), the palm's length (cm) and the arm's reach about the shoulder (cm), plus the reach box in centimetres. If you share your results, only those three numbers and the name of the camera model each shared game round used are included; nothing else about your face.

**Replay log.** While the page is open it keeps a log of the last few minutes: the 21 hand points the tracker found in each frame (as numbers), the cursor, pinches, the targets and messages on screen. It contains no picture. It lives only in that browser tab and is gone when you reload or close it. It leaves your computer only if you press “Download replay log” and send the file to someone yourself.

**What is saved.** A profile of a few numbers (how far your hand comfortably reaches, your pinch threshold, how much the cursor wobbles, how strongly buttons should pull the cursor in, your answers and ratings) is saved in your browser's local storage. It stays in that browser unless you download it or share it. "Reset to defaults" deletes it. The page also keeps a private history of your sessions for each hand (accuracy, time, how easy you rated each step), so it can compare your right and left hand over time; that history is not included when you share unless you tick "Include my last game rounds" on the sharing screen, which adds the scores, counts and precision numbers of your last few game rounds, plus which hand model each round used and, for adaptive rounds, how many times each model was chosen (never any camera data); "Delete history" on the comparison screen removes it.

**Sharing is optional and public.** If you press "Share results with the project", the page opens a GitHub issue pre-filled with those numbers, your browser type and screen size. Nothing is sent until you press Submit on GitHub. The issue is public, is posted under your GitHub username, and GitHub's own privacy policy applies. You need a GitHub account, which GitHub offers to people 13 and older. Students at a school should check with a teacher or parent before sharing. A bot stores the numbers in this repository, where anyone can read them, and closes the issue. If you tick "Include my last game rounds", the share also carries the last few game rounds as numbers (scores, accuracy, placement error, slips), the hand model each round used, which trial features were on, and how often each was in play; nothing else. To have a shared result removed, open an issue saying which result number.

**Third parties your browser contacts.** Loading the page fetches the hand-tracking library from jsDelivr and its model from Google's storage, and fonts from Google Fonts; those services see your IP address like any website you visit. The page itself has no analytics, cookies or accounts.

**Not a medical test.** This page adjusts a game-like control. It does not measure, assess or diagnose anything about your health, coordination, tremor or motor skills, and its numbers should not be used for that.

**No warranty.** The page and the atlas are educational tools provided as they are, without any warranty.
