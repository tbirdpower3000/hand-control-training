Hand control training v0.13 · 3 Nov 2026

About a minute of exercises that teach the Planetary Modeling Atlas how your hand moves, so the cursor reaches the whole screen, your pinch is read reliably, and buttons are as easy to hit as you need.

  1. Reach: move your open hand to four comfortable corners.
  2. Pinch: open your hand, then pinch thumb to index finger, a few times.
  3. Hold still: keep the cursor on a dot for three seconds.
  4. Targets: press eight buttons as they appear.
Which hands should it learn?

Each task says which hand to use. The camera checks it is that hand, and each hand gets its own settings in the atlas.

Sounds

With sounds on, two falling notes mean your hand has left the camera's view and a rising note means it's back, so you can shrink or hide the camera preview (slider at the bottom left during training).

After each step you can rate how it went from 1 to 7, or let it carry on by itself. Sit where you'll use the atlas.

Before you start The camera picture is processed on this computer by the hand-tracking library and never leaves it. No video or photos are saved. Your profile (a few numbers) is saved in this browser only, where the atlas picks it up. Sharing it with the project at the end is optional and needs a GitHub account. This is an educational tool for adjusting a game-like control. It is not a medical test and does not measure or diagnose anything about your health or motor skills.

Share your results?

Shared results help the atlas choose better starting settings for everyone, especially people who find hand control hard. A bot in the project combines them into shared defaults.

What is sent, and where Only the numbers below: measurements, your answers, your ratings, and a browser type. No video, pictures, names or email addresses. It is posted as a public issue on the project's GitHub page under your GitHub account, so your GitHub username will be attached to it and anyone can read it. You need a GitHub account (13 or older), and you press the final Submit button on GitHub yourself. Students under 13, or anyone at a school, should check with a teacher or parent first. If you'd rather not, just go back: nothing is sent unless you submit the issue.
